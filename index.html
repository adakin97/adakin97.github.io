Running Earth observation · OSINT

Damage Maps

Assessing structural damage in conflict zones is slow and dangerous on the ground, and cloud cover defeats optical satellites. Radar sees through both. This pipeline runs on Google Earth Engine: a NASA FIRMS thermal feed flags candidate sites within hours of an event, then the PWTT change-detection algorithm, developed at UCL, tests Sentinel-1 radar time series for structural change. I built the ingestion, processing, validation and mapping around the algorithm.

Applied across seven areas, including Bakhmut, Tehran, Natanz and Al Udeid. At one site it surfaced structural change that had not been reported in open sources at the time of analysis.

Ask me about: radar backscatter versus optical, why validation is the hard part, and where a person still beats the algorithm.

PrototypeRetrieval-augmented AI · Day job

A library assistant that answers only from its sources

An international NGO network I work for holds around twenty years of security guidance: about 1,250 guides, toolkits and research papers. People struggle to find the right one unless they already speak the vocabulary. I am building an assistant that takes a question in plain words, in English, French, Spanish or Arabic, searches the library and answers only from what it finds, with a link to every source.

What I am building

  • A catalogue of the whole public library, collected politely from the site's sitemaps: type, date, language, authorship and files for every item.
  • Automatic flags for guidance that may be out of date, split into what the organisation can update and what it can only label as historical.
  • A working prototype: keyword search (BM25) over resource descriptions and the full text of the core guides, then a language model that writes from the passages found and cites them.

What I am testing

  • 53 test questions covering finding a resource, explaining a term, other languages, sensitive questions, emergencies and out-of-scope requests.
  • Three build routes side by side: our own build, a website plugin and a hosted product, judged on answer quality first and on whether someone other than me could run it.
  • Safety rules: an emergency gets a fixed message, never a generated one, and the assistant points to guidance rather than advising on a live situation.
Concept note
Problem
Search depends on knowing the right terms. A field manager typing "staff robbed at a checkpoint" will not find a guide filed under access and armed robbery.
Approach
Retrieval-augmented generation rather than training a model: the library is searched at question time, so new guidance counts as soon as it is published and every answer can be checked against its source.
Safeguards
Public content only in the first version; every claim cited; no advice on live situations; questions logged briefly and without personal details.
Running it
Designed to need four to six days of upkeep a year, with a step-by-step runbook so a non-developer can own it.
Next
Real member questions to replace my drafts, the route test, then a build in early 2027.

TestingRisk modelling · Day job

Testing a model of what security spending returns

A colleague built a prototype that estimates the return on an NGO's security spending: how much expected loss a set of measures avoids, against what they cost, with ranges from a Monte Carlo simulation. The model is theirs. My part is to test it: run its maths in code, see which inputs move the answer, and write up what should change.

What I am testing

  • The expected-loss core, the gamma-Poisson updating that blends reference rates with an organisation's own records, and the 2,000-run simulation.
  • Edge cases: whether a bad year wrongly raises the return, and how much the result rests on placeholder values.

What I found

  • The headline figure should be the simulation's middle run, not a single central calculation: in the worked example they differ.
  • Overlapping measures can double-count, and several inputs carry no range at all, so the uncertainty shown is too narrow.
Concept note
Problem
Security budgets are hard to defend because the return is losses that never happened, and nobody ever observes it.
Approach
An expected-loss model in the Open FAIR family, reported as ranges. The closest everyday comparison is insurance: it costs a little in a typical year and saves a lot in a bad one.
My contribution
Review, testing and a written list of eleven fixes, three of them recommended first. A beta with the fixes was built with Claude Code for the author to take or leave.
Next
Agree the fixes with the author, then calibrate reference rates against open incident data.

In progressData governance · Day job

Mapping an organisation's data, then cleaning it up

Before any AI tool touches an organisation's data, someone has to know where that data lives and how it moves. I am mapping the systems behind a membership network: its CRM, forms, mailing tool and spreadsheets, and fixing how contacts and organisations are categorised so that its impact indicators can actually be counted.

What I am building

  • A data map that doubles as the organisation's UK GDPR record of processing.
  • An improvements register: every possible fix with a stable ID, the evidence behind it and its status.
  • A new categorisation scheme for contacts and organisations, agreed with the team and migrated in bulk.

How I am doing it

  • Structure, never records: I work from exports of the CRM's objects, fields and links, not from member data.
  • Changes go through sign-off and are tested on a few records before the rest, with a backup export as the undo.
Concept note
Problem
Categories drift, duplicate score fields multiply, and reports stop matching each other. Any automation built on top inherits the mess.
Approach
Rules before models: document the fields, agree definitions, fix the counting, and only then automate.
Next

In useLearning · Personal

Peripatos: a learning grove for what I meet each day

I have learnt a lot of maths, statistics and economics and forgotten more of it. Peripatos turns what I come across each day, at work or in reading, into short study cards, and grows each one as a tree as I recall it. The name is the covered walk at Aristotle's Lyceum, where teaching happened on foot.

What it does

  • A path of everything I have planted, in the order I met it, with forks suggesting what to learn next, starting with gaps underneath what I already know.
  • Daily walks of spaced repetition: plants come back after 1, 2, 5, 12, 30 and 75 days, and wilt if left.
  • Claude turns a note such as "gamma distribution, seen in a risk model" into a card and names the topics around it.

What is in it

  • 52 topics across maths, data science, economics and computer engineering, each with real-world uses, interactive sketches and further reading.
  • The trees are drawn by a seeded recursive function, so each topic always grows the same way.
Concept note
Inspiration
A friend's app for learning Chinese from what she was watching and reading.
Idea
Learning sticks when it is tied to where you met it and revisited on a schedule. The game is growth: recall makes a plant grow, neglect makes it wilt.
Next
More topics, fresh questions written by Claude so walks do not repeat, and a teach-it-back mode.

How I think about data

The projects differ. The discipline behind them does not.

Rules before models

At Bloomberg I used a scikit-learn classifier to analyse a framework of 5,000+ business rules and work out which should survive a data-model migration. The lesson stuck. Most data quality problems are governance problems, and a model only helps once you understand the rules it sits on.

Humans in the loop, on purpose

A good pipeline decides what a machine should do and where a person must check. The ingestion workflow I built at Bloomberg routed exceptions to analysts rather than passing them silently. My damage pipeline flags candidate sites automatically but nothing gets called damage until a person has looked. Knowing what not to automate is the skill.

Validation is the deliverable

A detection nobody can trust is noise. The interesting work is the checking: baselines, false positives, and what the sensor cannot see. This page says "in build" wherever something is unfinished, because a claim without evidence behind it costs more than it earns.

Write it down

Data only changes decisions when someone who does not write code can act on it. At Bloomberg I was the main point of contact for the EMEA ESG loans dataset and turned it into written market research, including two editions of LMA Horizons read by banks and asset managers.
